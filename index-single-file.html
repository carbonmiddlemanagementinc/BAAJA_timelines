How much sooner under BAAJA?

The time savings in the bill come largely when reviews run into delays and challenges, and the largest savings are likely to come for transmission.

The bill has a range of potential time savings across different energy infrastructure projects

Each archetype is meant to be illustrative of how BAAJA could change project timelines and is a composite of recorded cases. They are not representative of projects currently proposed or being built. These savings are not guaranteed across all projects. These savings count only months. They do not count projects that current law stopped and the bill might have let proceed.

What's included

144 past projects: every project-specific EIS in CEQ's 2010 to 2024 data that fits the selection rule for these six project types, plus Crimson Solar, three reviews still under way and 25 BLM geothermal exploration reviews. The 77 candidates left out, and why, are in a CSV.

How the replay works

Each project's recorded dates are replayed under the bill's limits: the EIS capped at 28 months from the NOI (the 2-year deadline plus a 4-month sponsor suit), the decision at 2 months after the final EIS, BLM's wait before the NOI at the bill's deadlines, and test-well permits at 2 months. Months the bill would excuse or not cut, such as a developer's redesign or a funding lapse that began before a deadline, stay in.

How to read it

Each row is one project, sorted by months saved. The grey bar is the recorded review, from the NOI (or, where the replay counts a wait before the NOI, the start of that wait) to the federal decision; the blue bar below ends where the decision would have come under BAAJA, and the chip by the name gives the difference. Hover or tap a bar for its dates and why it ran long.

Project type

The replays stop at the federal decision, because the records do not show when projects were built. They assume agencies and developers behave as they did, except that developers refuse extensions they did not cause; on the Permitting Dashboard developers usually agreed to them. Reviews still under way use the agency's current schedule. Every project, with its sources, is in the CSV.

Key assumptions and how they compare

No published analysis estimates months saved per project from deadline enforcement, a 150-day filing limit or remand without vacatur, so the figures on this page are its own estimates. The table shows where the key assumptions sit against the published record.
